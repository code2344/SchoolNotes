# Acids

Acids produce hydrogen ions, $\mathrm{H^+}$, when dissolved in water and usually have a pH below 7. Some acids are molecular substances that ionise in water; they are not all ionic compounds.

Indicators change colour depending on pH and can be used to identify acidic or basic solutions.

## Acid reactions

- acid + metal → salt + hydrogen
- acid + base → salt + water
- acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide

For example:

$$2\mathrm{HCl}_{(aq)} + \mathrm{Mg}_{(s)} \rightarrow \mathrm{MgCl}_{2(aq)} + \mathrm{H}_{2(g)}$$

The metal forms the positive ion in the salt, while the acid supplies the negative ion. See [[Bases and Neutralisation]], [[Ions]] and [[Ionic Formulas]].
