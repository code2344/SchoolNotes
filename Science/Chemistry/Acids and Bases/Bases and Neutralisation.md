# Bases and Neutralisation

A base neutralises an [[Acids|acid]]. Soluble bases are called alkalis and produce hydroxide ions, $\mathrm{OH^-}$, in water. Alkaline solutions have a pH greater than 7.

For example, sodium hydroxide dissociates in water:

$$\mathrm{NaOH}_{(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Na^+}_{(aq)} + \mathrm{OH^-}_{(aq)}$$

Common bases include sodium hydrogen carbonate ($\mathrm{NaHCO_3}$), potassium hydroxide ($\mathrm{KOH}$) and ammonia ($\mathrm{NH_3}$).

Neutralisation follows this general pattern:

**acid + base → salt + water**

$$2\mathrm{HCl}_{(aq)} + \mathrm{Mg(OH)}_{2(s)} \rightarrow \mathrm{MgCl}_{2(aq)} + 2\mathrm{H_2O}_{(l)}$$

The worked sodium hydroxide example is in [[Hydrochloric Acid and Sodium Hydroxide]]. These equations must follow [[Conservation of Mass and Chemical Equations|conservation of mass]].
