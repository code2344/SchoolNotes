# Ionic Formulas

Ionic compounds contain positive and negative [[Ions|ions]] in a ratio that gives the compound no overall charge.

| Step | Magnesium chloride example |
| --- | --- |
| Identify the cation and anion | Magnesium ion and chloride ion |
| Write their charges | $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ |
| Balance the total charge | One magnesium ion requires two chloride ions |
| Write the formula | $\mathrm{MgCl_2}$ |

Acid–carbonate reactions follow the pattern described in [[Acids]]:

$$2\mathrm{HCl}_{(aq)} + \mathrm{CaCO}_{3(s)} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaCl}_{2(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2O}_{(l)} + \mathrm{CO}_{2(g)}$$

$$\mathrm{H_2SO}_{4(aq)} + \mathrm{CaCO}_{3(s)} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaSO}_{4(s)} + \mathrm{H_2O}_{(l)} + \mathrm{CO}_{2(g)}$$

Both equations obey [[Conservation of Mass and Chemical Equations|conservation of mass]].
